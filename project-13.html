Overview

Kill Web is a real-time command game that runs in the browser. The player finds every track, identifies it, then pairs the right shooter, because nothing fires until a track is identified. It has nine missions on the fictional Kestrel Coast and five pace levels that speed the enemy up faster than the player. The same project documents three CAD assemblies modeled in Python and inspected in a custom three.js viewer, and two interactive isometric anatomy figures. Every game value is fictional, and the CAD renders show exterior shape only.

How the Game Works

The simulation advances in fixed 0.25 s steps and never touches the DOM or WebGL, so the same code drives the browser game and a headless Node test harness. The client is 6,212 lines of JavaScript in 10 ES modules with no framework or build step. three.js renders each mission's 48 x 38 km map as a 640 x 507 height mesh under a 4,096 x 3,243 ground texture, with 1,491 to 6,377 instanced buildings per mission, all generated from the simulation's own terrain functions. Sound is synthesized at runtime, so the whole game is a 78-file, 36.8 MB static site.

Pace replaces unlock grinding: the full arsenal is available from mission 1, and at the top level raids arrive 1.75 times as often and fly 1.45 times as fast while friendly deployment speeds up by 1.4. A scripted commander plays every mission headless at every pace with 10 fixed seeds; the latest 450-run sweep wins 92.7% overall (87.8% to 98.9% by pace), and median mission time falls from 36 to 22 game minutes between the first and fifth pace levels. The harness caught design faults before players did, including a convoy mission that got easier at higher pace.

CAD Studies

  • Zenith-6 tube-launched UAS, representative model of a publicly shown aircraft: 60 unique parts, 150 instances, checked in flight and stowed configurations.
  • Wrath collaborative combat aircraft, representative model of a publicly shown aircraft: 125 unique parts, 130 instances.
  • Shade-X quiet ISR helicopter, original home-built design: 93 unique parts, 142 instances.

Every part is a parametric B-rep solid in CadQuery 2.8 on Open CASCADE 7.9, numbered by subsystem. All three assemblies report 0 clashes and 0 floating parts; the two fixed-wing models were confirmed with a two-stage check that screens every instance pair as meshes at 0.05 mm deflection, then re-tests candidates with exact B-rep booleans. Each assembly ships as one self-contained HTML viewer with orbit, standard views, explode, a live section plane and coloring by material or subsystem.

Only exterior renders are published. Parts lists, masses, materials and internal layouts stay private, and the Wrath render omits its underwing stores.

Isometric Anatomy Figures

Two interactive SVG figures, drawn in a fixed isometric projection with four-tone shading and back-to-front painter's ordering, show how the Shade-X helicopter field kit (150 moving part groups in 10 assemblies, with WebGL rotor effects) and the Zenith-6 launch test deck (151 part groups, 13 explode steps) come apart.

Legal and Sourcing

Kill Web and these studies are an unofficial, non-commercial fan project. They are not affiliated with, endorsed or sponsored by Anduril Industries, Inc. or any other company. Every unit, system and place name in the game, and the names of the CAD studies and figures, are fictional.

No company logos, photographs, documents or data are used. Game terrain, art, unit models and sound are generated by code in this project; two of the CAD models are representative studies of publicly shown aircraft, approximated from public photographs and published overall dimensions; every performance value in the game is fictional, and hangar notes describe real-world classes of systems with links to public reporting.
